Cylinder Ends & Stuffing

Why make a cylinder?

The cylinder is the second most essential shape in amigurumi construction. While spheres make great heads and rounded bodies, cylinders form the structural backbone for limbs, necks, torsos, and specialized accessories.

Classic & Primary Uses

Amigurumi Limbs & Features:

  • Arms, Legs, and Tails: Straight or slightly tapered tubes form animal legs, doll arms, tails, and tentacles.
  • Necks & Torsos: Long or stout cylindrical sections build structural necks and main bodies for standing figures.

Clothing & Accessories:

  • Sweater sleeves, wizard hats, tree trunks, plant stems, or cylindrical mugs and bottles.

Amigurumi Yarn & Hook Guide

What type of yarn should I use specifically for Amigurumi?

Standard crochet patterns (like blankets or garments) use larger hooks to create a soft, flexible drape. Amigurumi requires a tight, dense fabric so the fiberfill stuffing doesn't poke out through the stitches. The golden rule for amigurumi is to drop 1 to 3 hook sizes (0.5 mm - 1.5 mm smaller) than what the yarn skein label recommends.

3 Rules of Thumb for Choosing Your Hook

  • Check for "Gapping": If you stuff your piece and can see white stuffing poking between your single crochet stitches, drop down 0.5 mm in hook size.
  • Yarn Fiber Matters:
    • 100% Cotton: Zero stretch. You can stick closer to a 3.0 mm – 3.5 mm hook for worsted weight.
    • Acrylic: Slight give/stretch. Usually pairs best with a 3.5 mm – 4.0 mm hook for worsted weight.
    • Chenille / Velvet Yarns: Notoriously tricky because pulling stitches too tight can cause "snagging" or breaking the core thread. Using a 5.0 mm – 6.5 mm hook is usually the sweet spot to keep it plush without losing shape.
  • Stitch Tension & Style: If you use the "Yarn Under, Yarn Over" (X-stitch) method instead of standard "Yarn Over, Yarn Over" (V-stitch), your stitches naturally sit tighter, allowing you to use a slightly larger hook while keeping the fabric dense.

Cylinder Ends Guide: Open vs. Closed

Choosing whether your cylinder is open or closed depends entirely on how it connects to your project or if it functions as an independent standalone piece.

Cylinder Type Best Used For Construction Details
Closed (Flat Bottom / Top Closure) • Standing doll legs and feet
• Solid animal torsos or snouts
• Standalone cylindrical items (mugs, pillars, stems) 		Starts with a flat circular base (or magic ring increase rounds) and finishes with a seamless decrease round to completely close off the top or bottom.
Open (Tube / Limb Sleeve) • Sleeves for clothing or costumes
• Tentacles, floppy arms, or hollow handles
• Connecting directly into a body opening (no sewn-on base needed) 		Worked straight in continuous rounds from a starting chain or ring without any starting or ending decrease caps, leaving both ends open as a hollow tube.
