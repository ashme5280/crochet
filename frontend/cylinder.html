Why make a cylinder?

The cylinder shape is one of the foundational building blocks of amigurumi crochet, used to construct the core structure and functional parts of toys, dolls, and miniature objects.

Main Purposes of a Cylinder in Amigurumi

  • Limbs and Appendages: They are the standard shape for arms, legs, paws, and hooves because of their narrow, straight, or gently curved tube form.
  • Bodies and Torsos: Wider cylinders form the main trunk or body of animal figures, dolls, and creatures.
  • Necks and Connectors: Short, narrow cylinders serve as the neck that bridges a spherical head to a body.
  • Tails and Snouts: Long or tapered cylinders make flexible tails, snouts, horns, or ears.
  • Props and Accessories: Because cylinders can stand on their own or hold a hollow structure, they are ideal for crafting miniature hats, cups, barrels, baskets, plant pots, and pencil holders.
  • Finger Puppets: Small open-ended cylinder tubes are built specifically as the base for finger puppets.

What are Yarn Weights?

US System uses numbers (0-7) and descriptive terms (Fingering, Worsted, Bulky). UK/AU/NZ System frequently uses "Ply" (e.g., 4-ply, 8-ply). Note: In modern manufacturing, "ply" refers to thickness class, not literal strands.

Amigurumi Yarn & Hook Guide

For amigurumi, you generally want to use a hook size **smaller** than what the yarn label recommends. This creates tight stitches that prevent the polyester stuffing from showing through.
